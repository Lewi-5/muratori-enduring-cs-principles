## Why this week exists

Every later week measures or optimizes arithmetic on `double` values, and most of `geolab` is arithmetic on coordinates. A `double` is not a real number: it is one of finitely many binary fractions, and every operation on it is a rounding. Week 3 makes that concrete in two ways. First, the learner takes a `double` apart into its sign, exponent, and fraction bytes, then learns to compare and measure error honestly (ulps, tolerances, analytic reference values, and the difference between *the formula is correct* and *the formula is well-conditioned*). Second, the learner builds the small **geospatial math library** the syllabus asks for: degrees and longitude wrapping, unit vectors and coordinate bases, great-circle distance by three formulas, and planar segment intersection with its degenerate cases. The running example is the Haversine distance from the Computer, Enhance! interlude. The habit from weeks 1 and 2 continues: **predict before running, and record what the evidence does and does not establish.**

By the end, a learner should be able to decompose and rebuild a binary64 value and classify zeros, subnormals, infinities, and NaNs; explain why `0.1 + 0.2 != 0.3` and why addition is not associative without treating either as a bug; choose an error measure (absolute, relative, ulp) and justify a tolerance from an analytic bound or a measurement; wrap longitudes with the antimeridian policy stated; convert latitude/longitude to a unit vector and back, and separately express a vector in a new orthonormal basis; explain why a distance formula that is algebraically correct can lose accuracy for small distances; and classify segment pairs (crossing, touching, parallel, collinear, degenerate) with exact predicates on a stated domain.

Do not add later-week topics: generating or parsing datasets (week 4), a timing protocol (5, 13), SIMD or approximations of `sin`/`asin` (33–34), spatial indexes (43, 45), threads, or any speed comparison. This week makes **no performance claim**. Do not use the spherical model to claim geodesic accuracy: the answer key may state how far a sphere differs from an ellipsoid only with a cited source.

## Corrections incorporated after implementation

Implementing and verifying this week found the following errors or gaps in the specification above. The package follows the corrected statements; the original text is left in place so the change is visible.

- **`fmod` exactness citation.** The readings table said the `fmod(3)` manual page states the result is exact. It does not (it gives only the sign and range). The guarantee is C11 §F.10.7.1 ¶2, “the returned value is exact” (with §7.12.10.1 ¶3 defining the result as `x − ny`), checked in the N1570 text. The E03 explanation and P01(e) cite the standard.
- **The clamp does not always prevent a NaN.** E05 says `haversine_km` must clamp “so a valid input can never yield NaN”. For the `asin(sqrt(a))` form no NaN was reproducible: `a` does exceed 1 by one ulp (exact antipodes at integer latitudes 8, 12 and 82), but `sqrt(1 + 2^−52)` rounds to 1. The `2·atan2(√a, √(1−a))` form does produce NaN for those three. The clamp remains required by the contract. The observed lack of NaNs for one form does not prove removing its clamp safe for all inputs and environments; the answer key explains the rounded square root without calling it a tie. The 13×13 lattice of 15° steps never triggers the case, so the tests add integer-latitude exact antipodes.
- **Haversine is not exact at exact antipodes.** The E05 answer measured up to 1.9e−4 km (relative 9.5e−9) at L = 3 for `(L,0)`–`(−L,180)`, while the vector formulation is exact there; S02 measures the same effect at nearly antipodal distances. The contract asserts a derived bound (2e−8 relative), not accuracy.
- **Zero results are `+0.0`.** E03/E04 need `sin_deg`/`cos_deg` (and the unit-vector components) to return `+0.0` for zero, otherwise the required driver text `sin_deg(180)=0` prints `-0`. This is now part of the contract.
- **Endpoint exactness needs a test that can fail.** “Return that endpoint exactly” cannot be tested with integer or touching-endpoint inputs, because recomputation is also exact there. A non-integer T-junction, `p0 = (0.2, 2.3)` on the segment `(−0.8, 2.3)–(1.2, 2.3)`, distinguishes it: recomputation gives `2.3000000000000003`.
- **E05 `identical` coordinates.** The law of cosines yields exactly 0 at many latitudes (for example 12.5°) and a nonzero 9.5e−5 km at others (for example 40°). The driver uses (40°, 33.25°) so the conditioning effect is visible, and the answer says the effect is data-dependent.
- **S02 reference and units.** `180 − 10^−k` is not exactly representable, so the reference uses the actual double longitude (`δ = 180 − lon` is exact by Sterbenz), the angle is compared in radians before scaling by `R`, and the reference uses `long double`.
- **Tolerance justification.** For ulp-level constants (degree/radian round trip, `sin_deg`/`cos_deg`, normalization length, segment crossing) a ten-fold margin over one machine's maximum is not a meaningful criterion; each is justified by a derived worst-case bound instead, and lattice-level tolerances by a measurement at least ten times the maximum. The validation record lists which applies.
- **Time-consuming environment note.** Clang's sanitizer configuration takes about 275 s when the checkout lives on a OneDrive-backed `/mnt/c` mount and about 1 s per compile elsewhere; this is a filesystem cost, not a test property.

## Constraints carried forward from the week-2 review

The week-2 plan was reviewed and corrected before this one was written. The following lessons apply here and are checked in the acceptance section:

- **Verify every mathematical claim.** A prompt, stretch exercise, or answer must not assume a result it has not proved or measured. Where the plan says a formula “is expected to lose accuracy,” the answer key must derive the amplification or measure the error, and label measured values as measured. No digit count, error size, or ulp bound is asserted without a derivation or a recorded run.
- **State overflow and range contracts.** Every integer computation that can overflow has a bounded precondition or a checked failure, both for products and for sums, and no example relies on signed overflow. Floating-point overflow is well defined under IEC 60559, but each function states what it returns for overflow, NaN, and infinity rather than leaving it implicit.
- **State explicit limits.** Zero counts, narrowing conversions, accepted-parameter whitelists, and non-finite inputs are specified. A function that reports failure leaves its outputs unchanged (for arrays: validate, then commit).
- **Gate reference-platform observations.** Claims that rest on IEC 60559 binary64, glibc's `libm`, or x86-64 behavior are labelled and gated (`__STDC_IEC_559__`, `sizeof(double) == 8`, `CHAR_BIT == 8`, `DBL_MANT_DIG == 53`). A forced-skip build (`-DGEO_FORCE_NO_IEC`) tests the gating path; it does not claim to test another ABI.
- **Sequence side effects.** The order in which function arguments are evaluated is unspecified. No driver or test prints the results of two calls that share state inside one argument list; results are stored in separate statements first.
- **Workload honesty.** Learners receive typed, warning-clean TODO functions and supplied demo drivers; the learner implements the mechanisms, not plumbing. The estimate is unpiloted; a human pilot with per-exercise time and help logs is pending.

## Deliverable and working environment

The single graded assignment is a **geospatial math library notebook**: seven exercises whose functions are assembled into one small C library (`geomath.h`, `geomath.c`) in E07, their tests, and a report whose central content is *predicted versus measured error*. The implementation provides a learner package with prompts, typed warning-clean TODO functions, and supplied demo drivers; complete reference C and a full written answer key live in a separate instructor package. As in weeks 1 and 2, the answer key must answer every learner-facing “explain,” “predict,” “derive,” “compare,” and “interpret” request as well as every coding task. This file remains the specification for that work.

- Reference environment: x86-64 Linux (or Ubuntu under WSL), GCC and Clang, C11, `make`, the standard C library and `libm`. Python 3 only orchestrates tests. The build links `-lm`.
- Required development build: `-std=c11 -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic -Werror -ffp-contract=off -O0`, with an `-O2` build also passing every test. **Forbidden flags:** `-ffast-math`, `-Ofast`, `-funsafe-math-optimizations`, `-march=native`, and any flag that permits reassociation or fused multiply-add. GCC defaults to `-ffp-contract=off` in ISO mode and Clang defaults to `on`, so the Makefile sets it explicitly. Instructor verification adds an AddressSanitizer/UndefinedBehaviorSanitizer configuration on both compilers.
- E01–E06 are standalone `exNN.c` files, each with a supplied driver `main`. As in weeks 1 and 2, the public C harness includes each source with its `main` renamed, so named functions and types are part of the contract. E07 is three files (`geomath.h`, `geomath.c`, `geomath_demo.c`) compiled as separate translation units. Later exercises may copy helpers the learner wrote earlier. A supplied `support/geo_consts.h` provides only `GEO_PI` (a decimal literal; `M_PI` is not ISO C), `GEO_EARTH_RADIUS_KM` (6371.0088, a model constant), and the `Vec2`/`Vec3` type definitions. It contains no exercise answer.
- **Tolerance policy.** Transcendental results (`sin`, `cos`, `asin`, `atan2`, `acos`) are not guaranteed to be correctly rounded, may differ between glibc versions and between CPUs (some glibc builds select FMA variants at run time), and are never compared bit-for-bit. Tests use analytic reference values, exactly representable inputs, ulp distances, and stated absolute/relative tolerances. Each tolerance is justified in the validation record by a derivation or a recorded measurement, not guessed. Bit-exact expectations apply only to selected mathematically representable results, integer arithmetic, orientation predicates on the stated integer domain, and byte-level decomposition. Annex F makes `sqrt` correctly rounded: `sqrt(4)` is exact, whereas `sqrt(2)` is inexact. A transcendental result is assessed with a justified tolerance; correct rounding alone does not justify exact equality between different computational paths.
- Tests must not hard-code a libm output digit string. **Reference-platform checks** (E01 decomposition, the IEC 60559 relationships in E02) are labelled separately, gated to platforms defining `__STDC_IEC_559__` with a 64-bit `double`, and report “skipped” elsewhere. The sphere radius is a model constant; nothing here claims agreement with a real geodesic.
- Keep executable examples defined: no signed integer overflow, no invalid shifts, no reading a NaN's payload as meaningful, no `errno`-based math error handling, no dependence on unspecified argument evaluation order, and no assertion that a specific `sin` result has a specific last digit.
- The playground is the E05 driver: add a case (coordinates and expected distance), predict how many digits the three formulas agree on, run, and reconcile. It feeds the notebook rather than adding a second graded assignment.
- Learner submission: source files, the build file, test output, and `observations.md`. The report records compiler, `libm`, and flags, the tolerance policy used, and error tables, and separates **prediction**, **observation**, and **explanation**.
