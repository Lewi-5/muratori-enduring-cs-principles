## Tolerances and their justification

All tolerances are in [tests/tolerances.h](../tests/tolerances.h) and used by both the C tests and the Python driver. Two kinds of justification are used: **(A)** an a-priori worst-case bound derived from rounding, which the measurement then stays under; **(B)** a measurement at least ten times the maximum recorded. Maxima are from [measure.c](extras/measure.c).

| Tolerance | Value | Measured maximum | Ratio | Kind and reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `TOL_DEG_RAD_REL` (round trip `rad_to_deg(deg_to_rad(x))`) | 1e−15 | 2.2e−16 | 4.5 | A. Two constant roundings (≤ 2^−53 each, relative) and two product roundings: worst case 4 × 1.1e−16 = 4.4e−16. |
| `TOL_SIN_REF_ABS` (30°, 45°, 60°) | 1e−15 | 1.1e−16 | 9.0 | A. The argument `r·(π/180)` has relative error ≤ 2 × 1.1e−16 + 3.9e−17 (`GEO_PI` versus π) = 2.6e−16, so at most 4.1e−16 absolute for `r < 90°`; times `|cos| ≤ 1`; plus one libm ulp (≤ 1.1e−16): ≈ 5.2e−16. |

Additional asserted bounds in the contract suite, each measured and stated at the test: haversine within 2e−8 relative of `π·R` at exact antipodes (measured 9.5e−9, ratio 2.1) and the vector formulation within 1e−12 (measured 0). The 2.1 ratio is deliberately not 10: the haversine error there is `2·√(k·2^−53)` for a rounding of `a` by `k` half-ulps below 1 (k = 2 gives 3e−8 rad, about 1.9e−4 km), so the bound is derived, and the test exists to catch a NaN or a large error, not to certify a digit count.

## Mutation check of the harness

Each mutation was applied to a copy of the reference source and `make PACKAGE=instructor test` (GCC `-O0`) was run; the unmutated copy was confirmed to pass first, because a failing baseline would make every mutant look killed. **Historical result: 17 of 17 behavioral mutants were detected**: wrap upper bound open at 180; wrap by the naive `floor` formula; `-0.0` leaking from `sin_deg`; `sin_deg` without quadrant reduction; unscaled normalization; a flipped cross-product term; the `atan2` haversine form without the clamp; the vector path replaced by `acos`; `lon_delta_deg` without range validation; `approx_equal` accepting a negative tolerance; segment touching excluded (`>=` for `>`); the exact-endpoint branch removed (exposed only by a non-integer T-junction, added after this mutant first survived); a domain limit of 2^26; `ulp_distance` ignoring the sign; `−0.0` double-counted in the ulp key; a subnormal classified as zero; and an unchecked `fraction` in `compose_double`. Three mutants survived: removing the explicit NaN branch of `approx_equal` (equivalent for these comparisons: NaN comparisons are already false); widening the clamp's upper bound in the `asin(sqrt(a))` form (survived these samples because `sqrt(1 + 2^−52)` rounds to 1, without a tie; this does **not** prove universal equivalence); and the `atan2` form *with* the clamp (a valid alternative implementation). Earlier rounds of this check found two real gaps, which the tests now cover: the haversine clamp was never triggered (the 15° lattice never hits an `a > 1` pair; integer-latitude exact antipodes now do) and the exact-endpoint requirement was never distinguished from recomputation.

## Limits

One machine, one glibc, two compilers. The tolerances and recorded errors are this platform's; a different libm may differ in last digits and should still pass every assertion, which is why nothing is compared bit-for-bit. No claim is made that the spherical model agrees with a real geodesic. Sanitizer flags (`-fno-pie`, `-no-pie`) are tooling choices for this Linux configuration, not C rules.
